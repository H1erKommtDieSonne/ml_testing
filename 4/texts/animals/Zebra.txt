Zebras ( US : / ˈ z iː b r ə z / , UK : / ˈ z ɛ b r ə z , ˈ z iː -/ ) (subgenus Hippotigris ) are African equines with distinctive black-and-white striped coats . There are three living species: Grévy's zebra ( Equus grevyi ), the plains zebra ( E. quagga ), and the mountain zebra ( E. zebra ). Zebras share the genus Equus with horses and asses , the three groups being the only living members of the family Equidae . Zebra stripes come in different patterns, unique to each individual. Several theories have been proposed for the function of these patterns, with most evidence supporting them as a deterrent for biting flies. Zebras inhabit eastern and southern Africa and can be found in a variety of habitats such as savannahs , grasslands , woodlands, shrublands , and mountainous areas. Zebras are primarily grazers and can subsist on lower-quality vegetation . They are preyed on mainly by lions , and typically flee when threatened but also bite and kick. Zebra
